Sheet 01 / 05 — Overview

Recruiting Built on Relationships, Not Requisitions

Good morning, everyone, thank you for the time today. What I want to walk you through is a working framework for how I'd approach talent acquisition for commissioning engineers at Motz, at every level, from an intern walking in the door to a senior project manager.

The short version, before I get into any of it, is this: recruiting here has to be built on relationships, not requisitions. Commissioning sits at a narrow intersection of mechanical, electrical, and controls knowledge, and it asks a lot of a candidate's life, real travel, real disruption, so we can't treat this like a generic req to post and fill whenever a seat opens up.

This is a quick overview of the framework. I'd love the chance to walk through the full version with you after this.

Sheet 02 / 05 — Philosophy

Two Governing Principles

The foundation everything else is built on.

Everything in this framework comes back to two principles. The first is transformational, not transactional. That phrase actually isn't originally mine, a mentor of mine named Larry Mitchell gave me that advice years ago, and it's stuck with me ever since: we're not just filling a job, we're offering someone a real career and a real trajectory, and that has to come through in every conversation we have with a candidate.

The second is relational equity. This means we build relationships with strong candidates well before there's ever an open role, so that when a project ramps up, we're not scrambling and starting from zero. Trust earned early is what pays off later, whether or not a role happens to be open the day we meet someone.

Together, these two principles are the foundation for how I'd approach every other part of this.

Sheet 03 / 05 — Method

How We Engage the Market

Attract, don't chase.

So how does that actually play out day to day? The short answer is: attract, don't chase.

First, we have to be precise, not oversold. If we tell a candidate something about the role or the company that isn't accurate, that's how we end up with a mismatch three months into a project. I'd rather under-promise and over-deliver than the reverse.

Second, I want us staying genuinely curious about motivation, not just qualifications. Understanding what actually moves a candidate, why this role, why now, matters more to me than closing them quickly.

And third, I want us reaching underserved talent well: strong candidates who are overlooked by a standard job posting or a generic outreach email, whether that's veterans transitioning out of the military, or people whose background doesn't look like a traditional CxE resume on paper but who bring exactly the skills we need.

Sheet 04 / 05 — Execution

First 90 Days

Building the system, not just filling seats.

In terms of execution, here's how I'd spend my first ninety days, and the goal here is building a system, not just filling seats.

First, I'd map the pipeline: understand where our current gaps sit against actual project demand, so we know exactly where to focus first.

Second, I'd build out the sourcing channels themselves, and align that outreach specifically to the project types that are driving the most hiring urgency, rather than treating every role the same way.

And third, I'd document the process. That means establishing real SOPs and tracking, so this system holds up and keeps working even as the scope of what we're hiring for evolves over time.
